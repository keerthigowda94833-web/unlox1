Learn about waste categories

Each waste category has a color and a purpose. Knowing the difference helps communities recycle more and keep dangerous materials out of the environment.

Recyclable

Materials that can be reused

Recyclable waste includes paper, glass, plastic bottles, cans, and cardboard. These items can be turned into new products when cleaned and sorted.

Example: newspaper, soda cans, plastic containers.
Biodegradable

Natural waste that breaks down

Biodegradable items are organic and decompose safely. Food scraps, yard trimmings, and paper towels help composting systems when disposed of properly.

Example: banana peel, leaves, coffee grounds.
Electronic

Devices and batteries

Electronic waste includes gadgets, chargers, batteries, and cables. These items contain valuable metals and harmful substances that need special recycling.

Example: phone, laptop, AA battery.
Hazardous

Dangerous household waste

Hazardous waste must be handled carefully. Paint, chemicals, medical supplies, and fluorescent bulbs can harm people and the ecosystem if thrown away incorrectly.

Example: cleaning solutions, broken light bulbs, medication.

Tips for correct disposal

Keep your waste practice simple with short reminders and local rules that help protect the environment.

Recyclable

Rinse containers before recycling, flatten cardboard, and keep items dry. Avoid mixing food waste with paper or plastic.

Biodegradable

Collect food scraps and yard waste separately. Compost kitchen waste when possible and avoid plastic-contaminated organic material.

Electronic

Drop off e-waste at certified recycling centers. Never throw batteries or electronics in ordinary trash bins.

Hazardous

Keep paints, chemicals and medical waste sealed. Follow local collection days and never dump hazardous liquids in drains.

Recycling Do’s

Do clean containers, separate materials, and follow your local recycling rules.

Recycling Don’ts

Don’t put greasy pizza boxes, used tissues, or tangled electronics in the recycle bin.

Local reminder

Check your community’s waste schedule. Some regions require specific days for hazardous and electronic collection.
